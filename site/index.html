Part 1

Branding basics: the rules before the ink

A decoration job starts with a logo file and a brand rulebook. Get these right and production is boring, in the best way. Get them wrong and every method downstream fails.

🧩 Logo anatomy

Most brands ship three forms. Mark (the symbol alone), logotype (the wordmark) and a lockup (both together). Small or narrow imprint areas take the mark; wide areas take the lockup.

📐 Clear space

Every guideline defines a protected zone around the logo, usually measured in units of the mark's own height (x). Nothing enters it: no text, no seams, no zips, no other logos. Use the slider below to feel it.

🔬 Minimum size

Below a certain size, counters fill in, thin strokes vanish and embroidery turns into a blob. Guidelines state a minimum in mm for print and a separate, larger one for embroidery. If the imprint area is smaller than the minimum, switch to the mark alone.

Try it: clear space = 1.0x

Drag to see the protected zone grow. On a product, the dashed box is the smallest imprint area that still respects the brand. Anything that lands inside it (a seam, a pocket edge, a second logo) breaks the guideline.

Colour language. Print shops speak Pantone (PMS) for spot inks, CMYK for full-colour process, RGB and HEX for screens, and thread charts (Madeira, Isacord) for embroidery. Always convert from the client's Pantone, never from a screenshot.

Ask for Production-ready artwork

  • Vector files: AI, EPS, PDF or SVG with text converted to outlines.
  • Pantone references for every colour, or a one-colour version.
  • The brand guideline PDF: clear space, minimum size, do-not rules.
  • Which version is allowed on dark backgrounds (reversed or white).

Reject Artwork that will fail

  • JPG or PNG screenshots, logos pulled from a website or a WhatsApp forward.
  • Gradients or photos for screen print, pad print, embroidery or laser.
  • Stretched, recoloured or re-drawn logos that are not from the brand team.
  • Text thinner than 0.5 pt for print or 1 mm for embroidery.
Why vector? Vectors are maths, not pixels. A screen, a cliché plate, an embroidery file and a laser path all need clean outlines that scale from a 6 mm pen barrel to a 300 mm tote. A raster file at 72 dpi has already lost that information and no tool can put it back.

Part 4

Where the logo goes

Standard imprint zones exist so that mockups, quotes and production all speak the same language. Pick a product, then click a zone to drop the logo in and read the rule.

Click a zone

📏 Measure from fixed points

Left chest: 7 to 10 cm down from the shoulder seam and centred on the chest pocket line. Centre chest: 7 to 10 cm below the collar. Sleeves: 2 to 3 cm below the shoulder seam. Measure from seams, never from the hem, because garment lengths vary by size.

🔄 Curves change everything

Bottles, mugs and pens are cylinders. Artwork must be narrower than the visible arc or it distorts and partly hides. Rule of thumb: imprint width up to one third of the circumference for pad and screen print; full wrap only with sublimation, UV rotary or a laser rotary.

🚫 Avoid the hazards

Seams, zips, pockets, buttons, drawstrings, vents on bottles, the handle line on mugs and the clip on pens. Any obstacle inside the clear space zone means the logo moves or shrinks, and the client must sign off the new position on the mockup.

Part 5

The real production clock

"Printing" is never just printing. Setup, flash, cure, cool, rest and QC all take time, and some of them cannot be rushed. This chart stacks every stage for one typical job of 100 pieces, one position, one colour, using typical mid-range figures. Hover a bar to read the stages.

Setup Decorate Dry / cure Cool Rest before pack or wash QC & pack
The rule of the rest. Water-based screen prints, DTG, DTF and pad-printed plastics keep curing after they leave the machine. Packing them hot, or washing them inside 24 hours, is the most common cause of a rejected batch. Build the rest into the delivery date.
